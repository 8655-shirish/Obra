- The existing `/admin/observability` dashboard calls `getCalendarObservability` through the existing `requireAdminMiddleware`. It displays scheduler owner/owner-only visibility, recorder registry uncertainty separately from shared response history, schedule/completion/outcome distinctions and bounded connection-action delivery review. Unknown recorder coverage remains visible even when no assessed thresholds fire; it must not show a previous recorder alarm as cleared. - `processCalendarActionNotices` runs from the monitor, not an owner request. No browser session, appointment, active checkout or fake appointment is needed. The action copy links to the real `/login` route and makes no delivery, booking-eligibility or refund guarantee.

## Alert Contract

Webhook envelope: `{source: "obra.calendar", owner, channel, alerts}`. Each alert has `environment`, `id`, `dedupeKey`, `status`, `severity`, `summary`, `value`, `threshold`, `unit`, `owner`, `channel`, `runbook` and `observedAt`. Dedupe keys are `calendar:<test|live>:<condition-id>`, never `bucket1-generation:*`. No webhook URL/token, recipient, provider payload, calendar contents or arbitrary diagnostic is in this envelope.

The configured adapter must upsert incidents by `dedupeKey`, deduplicate repeated firing observations and explicitly resolve on `status: "resolved"`. Successful assessments emit firing/resolved states only for assessable conditions; an omitted condition is **not** a resolution. SQL failure, null/malformed inventory, environment mismatch or missing required age evidence emits `assessment-unavailable` and does **not** resolve the last known conditions by treating missing data as zero. Keep prior conditions open/unknown until attributable evidence explicitly resolves them.

The legacy shared `obra-record-background-job-responses` may be foreign and invisible. Without an owned row, `response_recorder.registered` and `active` are both null: **registry unobserved**, not missing/inactive, and not an unavailable assessment by itself. An optional owned row yields `registered:true, active:<boolean>`; no new recorder is registered. A visible owned inactive recorder fires `response-recorder`; a visible owned active recorder resolves that configuration condition, not execution health. When both fields are null, omit the condition entirely so a previous alarm is not falsely cleared. Inconsistent pairs or `registered:false` are malformed, not evidence of absence.

`response_recorder.last_recorded_response_at` is `max(responded_at)` from the **shared existing** `background_job_cron_requests` ledger, at or before `observed_at`, including failed responses from any schedule/environment. The legacy recorder copies `net._http_response.created`, so this is response-created time, **not a collector heartbeat**. A null timestamp/no retained history does not prove recorder absence or inactivity. Even a fresh shared response cannot mask any calendar's stale/missing authenticated completions, off/failed outcomes, retained binding failures or aged paid obligations. The report/UI must display registry coverage and response history separately.

`connection:unverified` additionally reports stale unclassified provider-disconnected connections excluded from 140's normal recurring inventory. It is operator-facing, not a claim of revoked Google consent and not permission to email a reconnect demand.

When the existing oldest-100 overdue connection inventory is capped, positive breaches are emitted, but unsupported provider-condition resolutions are withheld. The inventory-limit alert makes this incomplete coverage visible; an unseen provider is not assumed healthy.

`binding_failures` is a separate uncapped reason-to-count aggregate from the existing binding rows, not a second incident store. It counts only the current connection/account/revision/environment, excludes disabled bindings and explicit pending/completed disconnect intent, and retains degraded failures during future retry cooldowns even when account reads are fresh. Fixed `google:binding:<reason>` condition keys remain firing after an idle successful invocation; the normal successful trigger projection clears the reason. Missing/malformed aggregates make assessment unknown, not healthy. Counts do not depend on the oldest-100 inventory and may safely resolve when that inventory is capped. The allowlisted reason is never raw `safe_error` prose; unrecognized/missing degraded reasons use `unknown`. These conditions route to the operator, not the contractor action-email classifier.

`<worker>` is `google`, `stripe`, `booking-core` or `booking-notifications`; `<provider>` is `google` or `stripe`. Numeric boundaries are inclusive. Healthy resumption resolves assessable condition keys on the next successful assessment; unobserved recorder and capped-inventory resolutions remain withheld. A generation error still determines the endpoint's original 500/503 status; calendar results are included alongside it. If generation succeeds but calendar configuration/assessment/send/settlement/webhook processing fails, the endpoint is 503 with safe calendar outcome. Firing operational conditions with a successful evaluation/delivery return 200; 200 does not mean there were no firing conditions or coverage gaps.

Core row failure totals are exact only when every lane supplies its `failed` total with no skipped/deadline-unknown outcomes. Positive `googleFailures`, `latePaymentFailures`, `recoveryFailures`, hold/expiry/segment details or lane failures still produce `partial_failure` and an unsuccessful family heartbeat when the full total is unknown (`failed:null`). The aggregate does not add detail counters a second time to a lane's total. A deadline or rejected lane reports invocation failure without exposing its error object. A healthy empty invocation can clear execution health, but cannot clear retained binding or paid-obligation conditions.

## Durable Authority

Unchanged migration 150 defines exactly five added columns, all on `calendar_connections`; the owner-scoped correction adds none and changes none of its business logic. No new table, queue, booking coupling or provider credential store:

| Column                                                  | Writer / Meaning                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| ------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `action_notice jsonb NOT NULL DEFAULT '{}'`             | Connection trigger and narrow RPCs. Empty before any incident. Contains only the current/retained incident and dispatch facts below.                                                                     |
| `action_notice_due_at timestamptz NULL`                 | Trigger/claim/settlement next eligibility. One-minute interrupted/unknown retry; unavailable recipient or paused temporary evidence gets five-minute cooldown. Terminal/review dispatches have no retry. |
| `action_notice_lease_token uuid NULL`                   | Random claim owner. Retained for exact idempotent settlement after a committed result. Not a login token.                                                                                                |
| `action_notice_lease_expires_at timestamptz NULL`       | Database-time 55-second claim TTL. A known accepted result retains the TTL so a simultaneous recovery/new episode cannot erase its response-loss settlement identity.                                    |
| `action_notice_fencing_token bigint NOT NULL DEFAULT 0` | Monotonic connection-local fence. Claim and unsent supersession advance it. Stale owners cannot authorize, fail or settle another claim.                                                                 |

`action_notice` delivery keys are `id`, `scope`, `opened_at`, `state`, and, when applicable, `closed_at`, `recipient_email`, `first_dispatch_at`, `payload`, `provider_message_id`, `accepted_at`, `review_reason`. Its required nested `current_incident` retains the current episode's `id`, `scope`, `opened_at` and optional `closed_at`, separately from an older frozen delivery. `scope` contains `source` (`saved`/`setup`), `cause` (`reauthorization`/`permissions`/`selection`/`setup`), `account_id`, `connection_revision`, `setup_operation_id` and `actor_auth_user_id`. Setup scope additionally has `setup_calendar_id` and `setup_selections_sha256`, covering sorted IDs/blocking/destination choices rather than display names or role observations. Profile/environment come from the connection row; replay deadline is `first_dispatch_at + 24 hours`. Payload/first-dispatch facts must exist together; JSON size, incident/delivery correspondence and vocabulary are constrained.

The incident begins on an actual classified current connection/setup condition. The trigger runs after the existing capability blocker, so read-only health cannot clear an outstanding write denial. A current incomplete configuration setup takes precedence over an old saved-account failure; temporary/setup-platform observations pause notices rather than request inappropriate OAuth. A targeted write-recovery probe does not create a second action incident for the same saved permission cause. A superseded cleanup probe's permission failure is not attributed to the owner's replacement account/calendar. Explicit pending/completed account disconnect suppresses that exact identity, including when provider-disconnected health itself would otherwise warrant a notice.

The same configuration and cause episode retains one ID through temporary observations. New successful verification with an advancing last-success timestamp, setup completion, supersession or disconnect closes it. A recurrent denial creates current incident evidence even if an older delivery remains unresolved. That old payload/notice/lease is never rewritten or resent to represent the new episode: delivery review still blocks another automatic email, but cannot hide or resolve the current incident. Late or duplicate acceptance settles only its original delivery. The admin table labels current incident and original delivery separately; no second notice table is introduced.

Recipient is the trusted `profiles.email` linked by `profiles.auth_user_id` in the same tenant/environment, matching that auth user's `email` with nonnull `email_confirmed_at` and no `deleted_at`. Initial setup also requires its recorded actor to remain that owner. There is no owner session lookup. Unverified/mismatched/missing recipients are visible review, not a guessed fallback. Neither `calendar_connections.external_user_id`, `account_email`, Google calendar ID nor provider display name is a recipient. Claim freezes the normalized address; preauthorization rechecks that owner/address. Payload bytes and cause/account/revision scope are immutable within the incident's provider replay.

### RPCs

All exposed functions below are SECURITY DEFINER with empty search path, grant EXECUTE only to `service_role`, and reject null/invalid environment. No browser or `booking_worker` grants are added. Raw service writes to the five notice columns are rejected even if it has old table privileges; legitimate writers execute as the migration owner through the narrow command. Existing owner-select RLS and provider/booking permissions otherwise remain unchanged.

```sql
claim_calendar_action_notice(p_environment text, p_lease_token uuid) returns jsonb

transition_calendar_action_notice(
  p_environment text, p_connection_id uuid, p_notice_id uuid,
  p_lease_token uuid, p_fencing_token bigint, p_action text,
  p_payload text default null, p_provider_message_id text default null
) returns jsonb

get_calendar_action_notice_health(p_environment text) returns jsonb
```

Claim returns null or `{connection_id, environment, notice, fencing_token, lease_expires_at}`. It locks with `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`, returns only one current intent, and moves expired ambiguous sends or missing recipients to review. It does not send.

`transition` actions are only `authorize`, `accepted`, `unknown`, `rejected`. Authorization verifies the exact incident/environment/lease/fence and current cause/configuration/disconnect/recipient before freezing payload. It returns `dispatch` with immutable `payload`, `idempotency_key` and `dispatch_budget_ms`, or `suppressed`/`deferred`/`review` with no send authority. Known results retry only this same DB transition (up to three calls), never the HTTP send. Acceptance after recovery/disconnect still records the actual result under the current dispatch fence.

`get_calendar_action_notice_health` returns environment/time, `action_required_count`, `pending_count`, `oldest_pending_age_seconds`, `review_count`, `setup_overdue_count`, `oldest_setup_overdue_seconds`, `platform_error_count`, `unverified_disconnected_count`, `items_limit=100` and safe `items`. Each item contains a `current_incident` projection (`id`, `cause`, `source`, `opened_at`, `closed_at`) alongside delivery-specific `notice_id`, cause/source/state, timestamps and `review_reason`. Open current incidents remain counted during temporary uncertainty; sending still requires a current exact remedy. No recipient, full payload, account ID, provider response, claim token or secret is returned. Delivery review and incident recovery are independent facts.

Private helpers: `calendar_action_notice_scope(public.calendar_connections)`, `project_calendar_action_notice()` and `guard_calendar_action_notice_write()`. No API EXECUTE grants. Existing types must include the five columns in Row/Insert/Update and the three exposed RPC signatures above. Do not generate a second notice table or widen the booking worker allowlist.

Retention follows the existing connection/tenant row. Resolved/accepted facts remain until a true new episode replaces that record after its settlement lease expires. Unresolved dispatched review is retained without automatic expiry or replacement. No separate archive or retention worker is added; deleting a connection retains the existing application's authorization/retention requirements.

## Provider Ambiguity

Resend documents a [24-hour idempotency retention window](https://resend.com/docs/dashboard/emails/idempotency-keys). The key is `calendar-action:<environment>:<connection UUID>:<incident UUID>`. Freeze `from`, `to`, `subject` and `text` as exact JSON bytes before dispatch; subsequent sender/template/config changes cannot mutate an in-window replay.

An unknown request/response/body can retry only within the original 24-hour window, with the same key and bytes, current scope and a fresh fence/preauthorization. Authorization subtracts one second from remaining TTL/window and the server subtracts authorization round-trip time using `performance.now()`. It never extends or resets first-dispatch time. A simulated 48-hour outage goes to `idempotency_expired` review with no second send, even though a provider with expired retention would otherwise accept that key again.

Known accepted results retry DB-only settlement. Failure to persist or confirm settlement is a monitor failure, not success or definite email rejection. Recovered/superseded dispatched work with unknown acceptance goes to `acceptance_unknown` review rather than suppressing away effect evidence. Provider 4xx rejection (except retryable 408/409/429) goes to `provider_rejected` review; an earlier ambiguous acceptance is still possible. Successful provider acceptance is **not** inbox delivery or Google invitation delivery. This path does not consume appointment-only webhook/review tables or fabricate booking notification events.
